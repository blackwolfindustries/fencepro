Confidential Franchisee Analysis

Top Rail Fence
FDD Comparison Report

2024 FDD (v1.2F, Issued April 19, 2024)  vs.  2026 FDD (v1F)
Major Changes • Red Flags • Areas of Concern • Prepared for Franchisee Review

The total investment required to open a single Top Rail Fence franchise has increased by $64,500 at the low end (57%) and $120,500 at the high end (56%) between the 2024 and 2026 FDDs. The largest single driver is the Initial Inventory requirement, which jumped from a range of $1,000–$5,000 to $45,000–$75,000 — an increase of up to $70,000. This is a material change that directly affects the capital a franchisee must have on hand before opening.

Critical note on "Security Deposits & Rent": In 2024, this could be $0 if you worked from home during the first 12 months. In 2026, a warehouse with an outdoor "lay down yard" of 5,000–10,000 sq ft is now required within 3 months (the grace period was shortened from 12 months to 3 months). This is a structural change in the business model, not just an inflation adjustment.
Critical note on "Initial Inventory": The 2024 FDD defined this as "post caps, gate hardware, bulk nails, and similar items" — small stuff. The 2026 FDD now explicitly requires purchasing common project materials like fence posts and pickets, costing $35,000–$60,000 of the range. The business model has shifted from just-in-time material purchasing to pre-stocking inventory.
Critical note on "Additional Funds": The 3-month working capital reserve has doubled. The 2026 explanation clarifies why: the franchisor now explicitly assumes you will have 1–4 employees (a salesperson plus a 2–3 person installation crew). In 2024, this was written for an owner-operated business. The staffing expectation has fundamentally changed.

7. Red Flags & Areas of Concern

🚩

Inventory Model Changed Without Disclosure

The business moved from just-in-time purchasing to requiring $45,000–$75,000 in pre-stocked inventory. This is a fundamental shift in how capital is tied up and how cash flows in the business. It was not communicated as a "model change" — it appeared as a quiet line-item revision.

🚩

Staffing Model Changed Without Warning

The 2024 FDD assumed an owner-operated business for working capital. The 2026 FDD explicitly assumes 1–4 employees. This doubled the working capital requirement to $60,000 at the high end. If you signed up expecting to run this solo, the economic model has shifted under you.

🚩

Affiliate Litigation with Troubling Counterclaims

A franchisee under an affiliate brand has alleged fraudulent misrepresentation, failure to deliver training and equipment, and breach of good faith. The same management controls Top Rail. The court denied dismissal, meaning these claims will proceed. This is not minor.

⚑

High Transfer Volume — Especially in Georgia

Georgia saw 18 franchise transfers in 2024–2025. This means franchisees were selling rather than operating. Before concluding this is positive (the brand is desirable), investigate whether these were exits driven by underperformance, personal hardship, or dissatisfaction with system changes.

⚑

Technology Fee Can Rise 25% Per Year

In 2024, there was no cap stated. In 2026, a 25%/year escalation is now codified. At $599/month today, compounding 25% annually: Year 2 = $749/mo, Year 3 = $936/mo, Year 4 = $1,170/mo. The FDD says increases above 25% are possible if new services are added. There is no absolute ceiling.

⚑

Insurance Costs Up ~54% at the High End

The annual insurance cost estimate increased from $9K–$12.5K to $10K–$19.2K. The 2026 FDD also discloses premium surcharges in WA, MN, and IL for specialty trade workers' compensation, and mentions monopolistic state Stop Gap coverage costs. Depending on your state, this could be material.

⚑

Growth Deceleration in 2025

After adding 75 units in 2024, the system added only 21 in 2025 — a 72% drop in new unit openings year-over-year. Possible explanations include market saturation, rising investment requirements deterring buyers, or franchisee performance issues reducing referrals. This warrants a direct question to the franchisor.

⚑

New Mandatory Bookkeeping Fee

The 2026 FDD introduces a $400/month ($4,800/year) mandatory bookkeeping fee paid to a third-party vendor chosen by the franchisor. There is no cap. While bookkeeping support has value, this is a new recurring cost that did not exist in 2024 and reduces franchisee autonomy over their own accounting.

⚑

Revenue Data Uses "Accepted Contracts," Not Cash

All Item 19 revenue figures in 2026 are based on "Accepted Contract" amounts — jobs signed, not necessarily completed or paid. Franchisees pay royalties on cash received. The difference between these two numbers can be significant if projects are delayed, cancelled, or disputed.

✓

Some Genuine Improvements

To be fair: royalties were cut by 1% across all tiers, the Brand Fund dropped from 2% to 1%, and Call Center fees fell from 2% to 1.5%. At scale, this saves real money. More financial data is also now disclosed. These are franchisee-friendly changes, though they do not offset the capital requirement increases.

8. Recommended Next Steps

Given the magnitude of these changes, here is what you should consider doing:

1. Request Exhibit L immediately. This is the list of every franchisee who terminated, was cancelled, did not renew, or stopped communicating with the franchisor. Call them directly — especially those in Georgia, where transfer rates were highest.

2. Consult a franchise attorney. If your original Franchise Agreement was executed under the 2024 FDD and the operating model has since materially changed (inventory requirements, staffing expectations, physical space mandates), you may have grounds to argue a material change was not properly disclosed. This is worth a legal opinion.

3. Ask the franchisor directly: Why did inventory requirements increase 14x? Were existing franchisees required to build inventory, or is this only for new franchisees? What happened in Georgia? What is the status of the A1 Kitchen & Bath litigation and does it affect Top Rail operations?

4. Get an independent financial review. Ask for the actual cash-basis (not Accepted Contract) revenue data for at least 10 franchisees in your year class. The difference between "contracts signed" and "money collected" matters enormously for cash flow planning with your new inventory investment.

5. Re-model your ROI. With a minimum investment now approaching $177,444 and median first-year revenue of $784,124 at roughly 6% royalty (plus 1% brand fund, 1.5% call center, $500/mo marketing, $599/mo tech, $400/mo bookkeeping), calculate your actual year-one net and compare it to what you expected when you made your decision.
